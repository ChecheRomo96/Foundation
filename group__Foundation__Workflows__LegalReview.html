Public visibility, a GitHub release, a downloadable archive, generated documentation, and a CMake package do not by themselves grant permission to use Foundation. The current LICENSE applies to source, headers, examples, documentation, build files, and binaries. GitHub-hosting rights that may be required by GitHub's terms are not treated here as a general software license.

Interim Guardrails

Until the review is complete:

  • do not describe Foundation as open source, freeware, public domain, or generally available for use;
  • do not grant an external-use exception in README, package metadata, release notes, email, or chat unless it is a deliberate written agreement;
  • keep LICENSE, README, generated documentation, and release-package text consistent;
  • do not accept third-party code contributions until ownership and contribution terms have been selected;
  • keep third-party test/build dependencies out of Foundation release packages unless their notices and redistribution terms are reviewed;
  • record every source or binary distribution already made, including its date, recipient, version, and accompanying terms.

These guardrails reduce ambiguity; they do not cure an ownership or prior licensing problem.

Questions for Counsel

Counsel should resolve and document at least the following:

  1. Ownership and employment obligations. Confirm whether any invention assignment, employment, contractor, university, client, or confidentiality agreement could cover the project. Record when, where, on whose equipment, and within which role the relevant code was created.
  2. Code provenance. Identify code copied, adapted, generated, or reimplemented from older personal projects, employers, clients, books, forums, libraries, or generated output. Preserve the source and license of every permitted third-party portion.
  3. Contributors. Confirm whether anyone other than the named owner contributed code, design, documentation, examples, or assets and whether written assignments or contribution terms exist.
  4. Prior grants and distributions. Review private shares, published Git history, package downloads, forks, messages, and earlier license files for permissions that may already have been granted.
  5. Dependency and notice obligations. Review build, test, documentation, and CI dependencies and determine which notices, source offers, or other obligations apply to distributed artifacts or generated documentation.
  6. Names and marks. Confirm that Foundation and any future repository or product branding do not create an unacceptable trademark or source-identification risk.
  7. Intended business model. Choose whether Foundation will remain proprietary, become source-available, use a recognized open-source license, or use a dual/commercial model. Define permitted evaluation, internal, educational, embedded-product, and redistribution scenarios.
  8. Warranty, liability, export, and jurisdiction terms. Select provisions appropriate to the intended users and distribution countries rather than assuming the interim disclaimer is sufficient everywhere.

Evidence Packet Checklist

Before the consultation, collect:

  • applicable employment, invention-assignment, contractor, NDA, and school agreements covering the development period;
  • a dated development history identifying personal versus third-party equipment, accounts, time, and repositories;
  • a list of imported or rewritten code and its original location;
  • the complete contributor/author list and any written assignments;
  • all historical LICENSE, README, package, and release texts;
  • the dependency inventory with versions, purposes, licenses, and whether each dependency is shipped;
  • a list of prior recipients, downloads, private distributions, and permissions communicated outside the repository;
  • the desired use cases and restrictions for Foundation, downstream libraries, firmware products, precompiled libraries, and source distributions;
  • the countries and entity/person that will own and distribute releases.

Exit Criteria

V1-003 remains BLOCKED until all of these are available:

  • written confirmation of ownership and authority to license the complete repository;
  • a completed third-party provenance and notice review;
  • an approved license text and distribution model;
  • approved contribution terms or a documented no-contributions policy;
  • consistent license metadata in the repository, documentation, package contents, and release process;
  • a recorded effective version/date and migration plan for already published material.

Replacing the interim notice is a deliberate future change. CI, test, and package success cannot close this legal gate.
